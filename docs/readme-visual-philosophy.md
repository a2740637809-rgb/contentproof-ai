# Evidence Modernism

Evidence Modernism treats information as a field of observable relationships rather than a stack of decorated claims. Space is disciplined and editorial: large quiet zones establish confidence, while compact evidence clusters reward close inspection. Every alignment should feel meticulously crafted, as if refined through countless rounds of expert review.

The palette is restrained to warm paper, archival black, cobalt decision blue, and a small amount of verification green. Color is never ornamental; it identifies decisions, linked evidence, and system state. Contrast must be calibrated with painstaking attention so the visual remains authoritative without becoming cold.

Scale creates the narrative. A decisive typographic proposition anchors the composition, while precise labels, reference numbers, and subtle rules suggest a research instrument. Rhythm comes from recurring modules and measured intervals, executed with the consistency of a master-level information designer.

Composition balances editorial publishing with technical observability. Human questions remain visually distinct from machine analysis, and connecting structures reveal how one becomes the other. The system should look labored over by someone at the top of their field, with every relationship clear before explanatory text is needed.

Typography is sparse and functional. Words act as evidence markers, not decoration; long explanations belong outside the visual artifact. The final expression should feel both human and inspectable—a meticulously crafted record proving that an AI conclusion can still return to its source.
